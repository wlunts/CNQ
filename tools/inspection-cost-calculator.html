The cost of China inspection service is typically calculated in man-days — 1 man-day = 1 professional inspector working on-site for 1 day at CNQ's standard rate of $188 USD/man-day. The number of man-days — and therefore your total inspection cost — depends on three key factors: product category (which determines daily inspection throughput), number of item types (more models mean more setup time), and (more units to inspect means more time and higher cost). Frequently Asked Questions

How do I use the inspection cost calculator?

Select your product category (toys, electronics, garments, hardware, etc.), enter the number of models and the sample quantity, and the calculator instantly returns the required man-days and total cost. Results show man-days, the $188/day rate, total cost, and multi-currency conversion.

How is the estimated cost calculated?

Model days = number of models ÷ daily model capacity; quantity days = sample size ÷ daily piece capacity. Man-days = the higher of the two (minimum 1). Total cost = man-days × $188.

Why do you take the higher of model days and quantity days?

An on-site QC can inspect several models in parallel across production lines, but each model still costs fixed setup and measuring time. The bottleneck — models or pieces — decides how many days the inspector really needs.

Why does daily capacity differ by product category?

The capacity table comes from hundreds of real inspections: standardized toys, electronics and hardware allow more models and pieces per day; garments need per-piece measuring and color checks, while furniture and ceramics need assembly and drop tests, so their daily limits are lower.

What is a man-day in product inspection?

A man-day (or person-day) is the standard service unit used to measure inspection workload and billing in third-party quality control. It does not simply equal a calendar day or 8 hours spent on the factory floor.

One man-day covers the complete end-to-end technical workflow:

  • Pre-Inspection Setup: factory liaison and scheduling, client spec sheet/checklist review, and custom inspection protocol preparation.
  • On-Site Execution: AQL sample extraction, workmanship verification, functional/safety testing, dimensional checks, and defect classification.
  • Post-Inspection Processing: photo sorting, data collation, formal report drafting, internal quality review, and post-inspection follow-up support.

Even if on-site physical testing finishes in less than a calendar day, the man-day rate accounts for all mandatory off-site preparation and reporting labor necessary to deliver a verified, actionable inspection report.

What determines the total man-days needed for an order?
  • Order Quantity & AQL Sampling Size: larger lot sizes require retrieving and testing more samples.
  • Product Complexity & SKU Count: orders with multiple SKUs, intricate assembly, or complex functional tests require more processing time per piece.
  • Factory Locations: goods spread across multiple manufacturing sites require separate man-day allocations per location.
What is the minimum inspection cost?

The minimum inspection cost is $188, which equals one man-day. Even if your order is small and the on-site work takes less than a full calendar day, one man-day still applies because the full service includes preparation, reporting, and follow-up work.

What is the typical cost for a quality inspection in China, and how should I judge the price?

In China's third-party inspection industry, pricing is normally quoted per man-day. Understanding the market range helps you avoid both unprofessional low-cost traps and overpriced services.

Standard market rate: $200 – $350 per man-day. This is the typical range for reputable independent inspection companies. It normally includes certified full-time inspectors, AQL sampling, travel within major manufacturing hubs, a detailed English report with photos within 24 hours, and integrity controls.

Low-price warning: below $150 per man-day. At this level, the risk of unqualified or part-time inspectors increases significantly. Some providers may not conduct genuine on-site inspections, or may produce unreliable reports. In the worst cases, inspectors may accept money from factories to pass defective goods.

Premium-price warning: above $400 per man-day. Standard inspections rarely need to cost this much. Unless your order requires specialized lab testing, complex engineering audits, or government certification, this premium usually reflects brand overhead rather than additional inspection value.
