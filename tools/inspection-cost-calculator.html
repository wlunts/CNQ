How Much Does Product Inspection Cost in China?

The cost of product inspection in China is typically calculated in man-days — 1 man-day = 1 professional inspector working on-site for 1 day (typically 8 hours) at CNQ's standard rate of $188 USD/man-day. The number of man-days — and therefore your total inspection cost — depends on three key factors: product category (which determines daily inspection throughput), number of item types (more models mean more setup time), and (more units to inspect means more time and higher cost). A simple hardgoods order costs as little as $188/man-day for 315 pcs, while complex electrical items may require multiple man-days — your product category directly drives the total inspection cost.

How to Calculate Inspection Cost?

  • Select the Product Category — each category has a different inspection pace and cost. General hardgoods: $188/day for up to 315 pcs. Complex electrical: $188/day for only 50 pcs.
  • Enter the Number of Item Types — multiple SKUs require switching between products, which increases inspection time and total cost.
  • Enter the Total Lot Size — larger lots increase your AQL sample size and inspection cost.
  • Choose the Inspection Level — General II is the default for most consumer goods. Special levels reduce sample sizes and lower inspection cost for low-risk scenarios.

Calculating your inspection cost upfront helps you: (1) lock in QC budget before production starts; (2) factor inspection pricing into supplier negotiations; (3) avoid cutting corners due to surprise costs. What Drives Inspection Cost in China?

Your total product inspection cost in China is shaped by five key factors. Understanding them helps you budget accurately and avoid surprises:

  • Product Category — determines inspection speed. General hardgoods: 315 pcs/man-day. Complex electrical: 50 pcs/man-day. Category choice directly drives your total cost.
  • Number of Item Types (SKUs) — each additional model adds setup and switching time, increasing man-days. More SKUs = more time.
  • Lot Size — drives AQL sample size. Larger orders mean more units to inspect, higher cost. Larger lots = larger samples = higher cost.
  • Factory Location — remote factories may require additional travel time, which affects the final quote. Remote locations may add travel time to your quote.
  • Special Testing — chemical analysis, functional tests, or safety checks add inspection time and cost. Special testing adds time and cost.
