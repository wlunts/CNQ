How to Use This Lead Time Calculator?

Enter four numbers and get a complete lead time timeline. The logic is simple — but it helps you quickly build a clear picture of your supply chain rhythm and plan inspection, booking and supplier communication.

What is Production Time?

Production time is the number of days a factory needs from receiving your order to completing production and packaging. Factory lead times are usually quoted in calendar days, weekends and holidays included. If unsure, ask the factory: "What is the exact date the goods will be ready?" — then calculate backwards.

How to Choose Transit Time?

Transit time depends on your shipping method. Below are general references. Always confirm exact days with your freight forwarder.

  • Sea Freight: 25–40 days from major Chinese ports to Europe/US
  • Air Freight: 7–12 days
  • Express: 3–7 days

Lead Time vs. Delivery Time: What's the Difference?

Many buyers use these two terms interchangeably — but they measure different parts of your supply chain.

  • Lead Time: from order to goods ready at the factory — the factory's job.
  • Delivery Time: from order to goods in your hands, covering both production and shipping — your perspective.

In short: lead time belongs to the factory, delivery time belongs to you. Knowing the difference helps you set realistic expectations against your sales calendar.

How Chinese New Year (CNY) Affects Lead Time

Chinese New Year is the single biggest disruption in the China supply chain — plan around it and you save weeks of waiting.

  • 4–6 weeks before CNY: factories fill order books fast and lead times stretch.
  • 2 weeks before: workers start heading home; output drops sharply.
  • 1–2 weeks after: workers return gradually; production ramps up slowly.
  • Rule of thumb: place orders at least 3–4 weeks before CNY and lock in factory capacity early.

Common Reasons for Factory Delays

Know the bottlenecks before they hit your schedule:

  • Raw materials not ready — one missing component stalls the whole line.
  • Tooling or equipment issues — mold repairs and breakdowns add days.
  • Labor shortages — most visible right after Chinese New Year.
  • Overbooked factories — popular suppliers split capacity across many buyers.
  • Rework after inspection — defects found on-site must be fixed before the goods can ship.

Why Buffer Time Matters?

Buffer time is the cushion you add to the entire production-logistics chain. Minor factory delays, inspection in China, port congestion, and customs holdups can all push actual arrival later than planned. 5–10 days is a common recommendation. Buffer isn't waste — it protects your sales rhythm from supply chain uncertainty.

How Does This Tool Calculate Dates?

Production completion = order date + production time. Arrival with buffer = completion + transit + buffer. All calculations use calendar days. Use this as a planning reference and check regularly with your factory and forwarder.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does forward mode work?

Enter your order date, production days, transit days and buffer days — the calculator returns the estimated production completion date and arrival date. Perfect for knowing when your goods will reach your warehouse after payment.

How does reverse mode work?

Enter the target arrival date and it works backwards to the latest order date, the required production completion date and the days left. Perfect for checking whether it is still possible to order in time for a fixed arrival deadline.

How many production days should I enter?

Typical ranges: standard electronics 15–25 days, garments 30–45, custom injection molding 30–40, ceramics and furniture 35–50. Use the upper end for first-time orders.

How do I estimate transit days?

Sea freight to the US West Coast 25–35 days, East Coast 35–45, Europe 30–40; air freight 3–7 days. Add 5–10 days in peak season (August–October, before Chinese New Year).

Why add buffer days?

Minor factory delays, inspection rework, customs checks, port congestion and weather all eat into lead time. Reserve at least 5–10 days; double it for peak season or sensitive goods.

What is the China time box?

It shows the current time and weekday in China and whether factories are in working hours, so you don’t message the factory at midnight and wait a full day for a reply.

Why does it warn about Chinese holidays?

Chinese New Year (usually late January to February) shuts factories for 2–3 weeks; National Day and May Day each close about one week. If your latest order date falls near a holiday, the result shows a red warning.

How accurate is the result?

It is a mathematically correct estimate, not a commitment. Confirm the dates with the factory in writing and keep about 10% extra margin on top of the result.

Can it help schedule inspection?

Pre-shipment inspection is usually booked 3–5 days before the completion date — the forward mode’s completion date is exactly the anchor for scheduling it.
