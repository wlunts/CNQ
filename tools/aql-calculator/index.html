About AQL Sampling

What is AQL?

AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) is the maximum percentage of defective units allowed in a product inspection. ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 is the most widely used sampling standard — the international counterpart of ISO 2859-1. The core principle of all AQL product inspection comes down to a single idea: a random sample is drawn from the entire batch. The number of defective units in that sample determines whether the full production lot is accepted or rejected.

Normal vs Tightened Inspection

This calculator uses the single normal sampling plan. Switch to tightened inspection (lower Ac) when quality history is poor, or reduced inspection when history is consistently excellent. Full switching rules are in ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 Section 9.

Why On-Site Inspection Is Irreplaceable

An AQL calculator provides statistical acceptance criteria, but only an experienced inspector can correctly identify defects and judge real factory conditions. CNQ inspectors additionally watch for: deviations from approval samples, packaging quality, workmanship consistency, and the production stages where factories are most likely to cut corners.
