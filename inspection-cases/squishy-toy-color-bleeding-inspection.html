Squishy Toy Inspection: Sticky TPR Koala Color Bleeding | CNQ

Published: 2026-08-20 路 Inspection Cases 路

On-Site Story: When "In-House Confidence" Meets Chemical Incompatibility

This final inspection serviceThis was a factory we knew very well 鈥?over the past year or so, our inspectors had visited their facility five or six times. To be honest, every time we went, we had a bit of a knot in our stomachs. While the factory had their own production lines, they routinely outsourced orders to smaller workshops during peak seasons. And the quality from those outsourced jobs was a completely different story from what they produced in-house. We had helped our client reject several batches already 鈥?not because we were trying to be difficult, but because the quality simply wasn't acceptable. So when this inspection came up, our inspector couldn't help but wonder: what would it be this time?

Upon arrival, the receptionist seemed to have read our minds. The moment they greeted us, they said with full confidence: "Relax 鈥?this entire batch was molded, produced, and packed 100% in-house, with QC monitoring every step of the way. And the product is so simple 鈥?just a TPR koala, a few plastic leaves, a couple of cotton balls, all in one bag. It's practically impossible for anything to go wrong! Today, you can rest easy 鈥?inspect as you like. There won't be any problems like last time."

Anyone who works with TPR knows that the material is inherently porous, sticky, and chemically reactive. Packing it in direct contact with cheap dyed plastic accessories is a ticking time bomb. Even if you don't see it immediately, after weeks of temperature changes and pressure during ocean freight, staining is almost guaranteed to happen.

The factory receptionist stood there frozen, speechless for a long moment. Finally, they stammered: "We calculated everything... how could we have missed this one?!" Our inspector could only smile wryly to themselves 鈥?yet again, the same story, the all-too-familiar "oversight."

This defect was found during our pre-shipment inspection of the sticky squish koala. Color bleeding is a severe aesthetic and chemical defect that often results in total batch rejection.
