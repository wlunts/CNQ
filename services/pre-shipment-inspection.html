What Is Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI)?

Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI), also known as Final Random Inspection, is an independent quality gate conducted when at least 80% of your order is produced and 100% of the goods are packed and ready. It is the most widely used product inspection service in China — and for good reason: it is the last chance to catch defects before containers are sealed and payments are released.

Certified inspectors randomly select samples from finished goods according to the internationally accepted ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 statistical sampling standard, then comprehensively review and test your product against agreed specifications. The result: you know exactly what you're shipping before the container doors close.

"We don't just check boxes. Our inspectors have rejected entire lots that factories swore were perfect." — This isn't about being difficult. It's about the judgment that 17+ years of on-the-ground quality control experience in China gives you.

Inspection Process — How It Actually Works on the Ground

You can refer to this general flowchart to see how third-party inspection companies typically operate. It's also what our inspectors actually execute inside Chinese factories every single day:

1

Order Intake & Standard Confirmation

The inspector completes this before leaving the office — confirming product specification sheets, client-specific requirements, packaging methods, AQL sampling level, and acceptance criteria. Without these, they don't step into the factory.

2

On-Site Arrival & Production Verification

First thing on arrival: verify production quantity has hit 80%+, all goods are packed and available, and the products match the order. If quantities are short or goods aren't ready, we don't pull samples — the clock doesn't start until the job is doable.

3

Random Sampling — AQL Standard Execution

Randomly select the specified number of samples from finished cartons according to ANSI/ASQ Z1.4. The inspector controls the entire sampling process — no factory-prepped "special samples" allowed. Use our online AQL sampling calculator to determine your required sample size before the inspection even starts.

4

Item-by-Item Inspection & On-Site Testing

Each sample is individually checked for appearance, workmanship, function, dimensions, assembly, safety, and more. Defects are photographed and documented immediately. On-site disassembly or function testing is performed when necessary.

5

Defect Classification & Pass/Fail Decision

Classify every defect as Critical, Major, or Minor per international standards. Cross-reference against the AQL acceptance table to determine pass or fail. This isn't a gut-feel call — it's an objective, statistically-driven decision.

6

Detailed Inspection Report Delivery

24 hours English inspection report (same-day for expedited services): sampling data, every defect found, on-site digital photos, defect classification statistics, and final PASS / FAIL / PENDING conclusion with professional recommendations. You now have "eyes on the ground" in China.

What To Check During a Pre-Shipment Inspection

A complete PSI report covers all of the following. This is not a pick-and-choose checklist — it's a systematic, comprehensive verification:

  • Quantity verification — total produced vs. available for shipment
  • Style, color & product description — match against order specs
  • Appearance & workmanship — cosmetic defects, finishing quality
  • Function & performance testing — does it actually work as intended
  • Dimension & weight measurement — tolerance against spec sheets
  • Assembly & construction — pre-assembly check for fitting issues
  • Packaging & packing method — inner/outer carton, protection adequacy
  • Labels, markings & barcodes — correct, scannable, compliant
  • Safety & reliability — sharp edges, small parts, stability risks
  • Client-specific requirements — any additional specs you've defined

What Quality Standards Used?

Generally third-party inspection company uses AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) tables for statistical sampling — the most widely adopted sampling acceptance standard in global trade. You don't check every single item, but through a scientifically-designed sampling plan, you can make an accept-or-reject decision on the entire lot with statistically controlled risk.

New to AQL? Read our plain-English introduction: ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 Sampling Guide for Importers. Then, use Online AQL sampling calculator to find your exact sample size in seconds — and estimate your required man-days and inspection costs in a single click.

How To Classify Defects

Every defect found is classified into one of three severity levels according to international standards. This is not subjective — each product category has a corresponding defect classification reference:

Defect Level Definition Impact
Critical Fails to meet mandatory regulations and/or affects consumer safety when using the product. Potential recall, legal liability, or personal injury
Major Likely to result in product failure, reduce product usability, or cause obvious appearance defects affecting salability. Customer rejection, returns, brand damage
Minor Not likely to reduce product usability, but may reduce salability or deviate from defined quality standards. Possible customer feedback but unlikely to cause returns

Based on the number of defects found per category versus the allowed figures in the AQL tables, we provide a professional recommendation to accept or reject the shipment. The final decision is yours — but we give you data-backed, on-the-ground intelligence to make it.

A Real Case: Pre-Shipment Inspection Catches Fatal Assembly Defects in Furniture

This is a real inspection case — a European furniture brand with an order of 2,800 units of flat-pack furniture, including TV stands, bookshelves, and storage cabinets. The factory claimed "all passed self-inspection, ready to ship." Our inspector arrived on site and randomly pulled 125 samples (per AQL Level II standard) for inspection.

What We Found

  • Screw hole misalignment — In over 40% of samples, pre-drilled screw holes didn't align with hardware at all. Our inspector spent 15 minutes trying to assemble one sample unit and discovered the door panel simply couldn't be installed.
  • Door panel holes drilled on wrong side — Several cabinet door panels had locker holes drilled on the left side instead of right (or vice versa), making doors impossible to install as designed.
  • Inconsistent hardware quality — Some batches of screws had incomplete threads or shallow Phillips heads that stripped instantly under an electric screwdriver.

Conclusion & Impact

Test results: 0 Critical, 42 Major, 18 Minor defects. The Major defect count far exceeded the AQL acceptance limit (under Level II, 2.5 Major AQL, the acceptance number is 7). The inspection verdict: FAIL.

What would have happened if the client had paid and shipped directly? 2,800 units arriving at a European warehouse — only for the end customer to discover they can't assemble the furniture. Returns, compensation, brand reputation damage. One pre-shipment inspection costing less than 0.3% of the order value saved this client from a potential six-figure Euro loss.

A factory audit in China would likely have uncovered the root cause — this factory's CNC drilling process and jig management had drifted out of tolerance. If identified at the supplier selection stage, the entire batch defect could have been prevented at the source.

What to Do When Your PSI Fails: A Practical Roadmap

A FAIL verdict isn't the end — it's your chance to correct course before the goods leave China. Below is the standard action roadmap we've developed from 17 years of on-the-ground experience. Each step is designed to put you back in control and ensure the factory bears its share of responsibility.

1

Pause Balance Payment

With CNQ's official FAIL report in hand, immediately pause your balance payment. This independent report is your strongest leverage in negotiations — without it, the factory will say "it's not that bad." With it, the conversation shifts from opinion to documented evidence.

2

Review Defect Severity & Feasibility

Work with the CNQ technical team and the factory to assess the nature of the defects: which ones can be reworked on-site, which justify a price reduction, and which require remaking. Don't make blind decisions — critical defects and minor imperfections demand very different responses.

3

Require Factory CAPA & 100% Sorting

Require the factory to submit a Corrective and Preventive Action Plan (CAPA) within 48 hours, explaining the root cause and corrective measures. At the same time, require them to arrange 100% sorting and rework of the entire batch — not sampling, every single unit.

4

Schedule Targeted Re-Inspection

Once the factory completes rework and passes their own internal check, schedule a targeted re-inspection. We'll focus specifically on whether the defects that caused the initial FAIL have been fully corrected. Per international trade practice, the re-inspection fee is paid by you upfront but can be deducted from the factory's balance payment.

Remember: a FAIL report isn't bad news — it's the last correction opportunity you get before releasing payment. Use it well, and you save more than money. You save brand reputation and customer trust.

Frequently Asked Questions — Pre-Shipment Inspection

How far in advance should I book a pre-shipment inspection?

We recommend booking at least 3–5 working days in advance. This gives us enough time to coordinate schedules with your factory, review product specifications, prepare tailored inspection checklists, and assign the most suitable inspector for your product category.

What is the minimum production status required before a PSI?

A Pre-Shipment Inspection should be conducted when at least 80% of the order is completed and 100% of the goods are packed and ready for shipment. If quantities are insufficient or goods are not fully prepared, our inspector cannot execute standard AQL random sampling.

How long does a pre-shipment inspection take?

For most standard orders, the complete pre-shipment inspection service cycle — encompassing pre-inspection technical preparation, on-site execution, and final reporting — is completed within 1 day.

The actual man-days required depend on the total order size, AQL sampling volume, and the complexity of on-site testing. Furthermore, because different clients have unique requirements, our technical team and inspectors must dedicate preparation time to fully align with your customized criteria before ever stepping onto the factory floor. Larger or highly complex orders may require allocating 2 or more man-days.

How soon will I receive the PSI report?

Your comprehensive English inspection report is delivered within 24 hours after the on-site inspection is completed. Same-day expedited reporting can be arranged upon request.

What AQL level do you use by default?

We follow ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 (ISO 2859-1) General Inspection Level II by default, applying AQL limits of 0 Critical / 2.5 Major / 4.0 Minor defects. These limits can be tailored to match your brand's quality tolerance and product standards.

What happens if the inspection result is "FAIL"?

If the shipment fails due to exceeding defect thresholds, we advise withholding final payment. The factory must sort, repair, or reproduce the non-conforming items. We strongly recommend scheduling a Re-inspection to verify the reworked batch before releasing funds.

Who pays for the Re-inspection if the first inspection fails?

Standard international trade practices dictate that the factory should bear the cost of any re-inspection resulting from an initial failure. However, to maintain our independence, we still invoice you directly. You can request your supplier to deduct the re-inspection fee from their final balance payment.

Can I customize the inspection checklist?

Yes. We build custom checklists matching your product specifications, artwork, packaging requirements, barcode scans, and special functional tests. You can also provide your own internally approved QC criteria.

Do I need to be present at the factory during the inspection?

No. Our qualified inspectors act as your local eyes and ears on the factory floor. We send you clear, photo-documented reports so you can make confident shipping decisions remotely.

How much does PSI cost, and are travel expenses included?

Our rate starts at $188 per man-day — all-inclusive, no hidden costs, no surprises. This covers all primary manufacturing hubs across China.

Can the factory pay for the inspection?

No. To preserve total impartiality and prevent any conflict of interest, we only accept payment directly from our clients (the buyers). This ensures our allegiance remains 100% with you and our reports are never influenced by the factory.

How do you prevent inspectors from accepting bribes from factories?

Integrity is our absolute bottom line. CNQ operates under a strict Zero-Tolerance Anti-Bribery Policy where all inspectors are legally bound by anti-corruption protocols with zero second chances. Furthermore, every on-site report and photo raw data undergoes mandatory secondary review by senior technical team members, and we provide factories with a direct, confidential compliance channel (compliance@cn-q.com) to report any irregularities.

Do I need to send you an approved "golden sample"?

For products requiring precise color matching, material feel, or subtle finishing details, sending an approved reference sample directly to our inspector is highly recommended. For standard products, digital spec sheets and tech packs are usually sufficient.

What happens if the factory is uncooperative or restricts access?

If the factory refuses standard sampling procedures, or restricts access to packed stock, our inspector will pause the process immediately and contact you from the site before proceeding. We never sign off on compromised inspections.

What is the difference between Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI) and Full Inspection (100%)?

The main difference lies in inspection scope, primary objective, and how defective items are handled:

  • Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI): Uses AQL statistical sampling to randomly select and test a representative number of units from the entire lot. It cost-effectively evaluates overall batch quality and provides a PASS/FAIL decision before final payment.
  • 100% Full Inspection: Every single item in the shipment is individually checked, with defective units sorted out and segregated on the spot. Ideal for high-value goods, zero-defect orders, or sorting after a failed PSI.

In short: PSI is statistical sampling — lower cost and faster. Full Inspection is piece-by-piece — thorough but more time-consuming and expensive. If your shipment requires total defect elimination rather than statistical sampling, our technical team can also execute dedicated 100% full inspection services on site.
