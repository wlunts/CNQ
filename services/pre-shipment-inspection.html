What Is Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI)?

Pre-Shipment Inspection (PSI), also known as Final Random Inspection, is an independent quality gate conducted when at least 80% of your order is produced and 100% of the goods are packed and ready. It is the most widely used product inspection service in China — and for good reason: it is the last chance to catch defects before containers are sealed and payments are released.

Certified inspectors randomly select samples from finished goods according to the internationally accepted ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 statistical sampling standard, then comprehensively review and test your product against agreed specifications. The result: you know exactly what you're shipping before the container doors close.

"We don't just check boxes. Our inspectors have rejected entire lots that factories swore were perfect." — This isn't about being difficult. It's about the judgment that 17+ years of on-the-ground quality control experience in China gives you.

Inspection Process — How It Actually Works on the Ground

You can refer to this general flowchart to see how third-party inspection companies typically operate. It's also what our inspectors actually execute inside Chinese factories every single day:

1

Order Intake & Standard Confirmation

The inspector completes this before leaving the office — confirming product specification sheets, client-specific requirements, packaging methods, AQL sampling level, and acceptance criteria. Without these, they don't step into the factory.

2

On-Site Arrival & Production Verification

First thing on arrival: verify production quantity has hit 80%+, all goods are packed and available, and the products match the order. If quantities are short or goods aren't ready, we don't pull samples — the clock doesn't start until the job is doable.

3

Random Sampling — AQL Standard Execution

Randomly select the specified number of samples from finished cartons according to ANSI/ASQ Z1.4. The inspector controls the entire sampling process — no factory-prepped "special samples" allowed. Use our online AQL sampling calculator to determine your required sample size before the inspection even starts.

4

Item-by-Item Inspection & On-Site Testing

Each sample is individually checked for appearance, workmanship, function, dimensions, assembly, safety, and more. Defects are photographed and documented immediately. On-site disassembly or function testing is performed when necessary.

5

Defect Classification & Pass/Fail Decision

Classify every defect as Critical, Major, or Minor per international standards. Cross-reference against the AQL acceptance table to determine pass or fail. This isn't a gut-feel call — it's an objective, statistically-driven decision.

6

Detailed Inspection Report Delivery

Same-day English inspection report including: sampling data, every defect found, on-site digital photos, defect classification statistics, and final PASS / FAIL / PENDING conclusion with professional recommendations. You now have "eyes on the ground" in China.

What To Check During a Pre-Shipment Inspection

A complete PSI report covers all of the following. This is not a pick-and-choose checklist — it's a systematic, comprehensive verification:

  • Quantity verification — total produced vs. available for shipment
  • Style, color & product description — match against order specs
  • Appearance & workmanship — cosmetic defects, finishing quality
  • Function & performance testing — does it actually work as intended
  • Dimension & weight measurement — tolerance against spec sheets
  • Assembly & construction — pre-assembly check for fitting issues
  • Packaging & packing method — inner/outer carton, protection adequacy
  • Labels, markings & barcodes — correct, scannable, compliant
  • Safety & reliability — sharp edges, small parts, stability risks
  • Client-specific requirements — any additional specs you've defined

What Quality Standards Used?

Generally third-party inspection company uses AQL (Acceptable Quality Level) tables for statistical sampling — the most widely adopted sampling acceptance standard in global trade. You don't check every single item, but through a scientifically-designed sampling plan, you can make an accept-or-reject decision on the entire lot with statistically controlled risk.

How To Classify Defects

Every defect found is classified into one of three severity levels according to international standards. This is not subjective — each product category has a corresponding defect classification reference:

Defect Level Definition Impact
Critical Fails to meet mandatory regulations and/or affects consumer safety when using the product. Potential recall, legal liability, or personal injury
Major Likely to result in product failure, reduce product usability, or cause obvious appearance defects affecting salability. Customer rejection, returns, brand damage
Minor Not likely to reduce product usability, but may reduce salability or deviate from defined quality standards. Possible customer feedback but unlikely to cause returns

Based on the number of defects found per category versus the allowed figures in the AQL tables, we provide a professional recommendation to accept or reject the shipment. The final decision is yours — but we give you data-backed, on-the-ground intelligence to make it.

A Real Case: Pre-Shipment Inspection Catches Fatal Assembly Defects in Furniture

This is a real inspection case — a European furniture brand with an order of 2,800 units of flat-pack furniture, including TV stands, bookshelves, and storage cabinets. The factory claimed "all passed self-inspection, ready to ship." Our inspector arrived on site and randomly pulled 125 samples (per AQL Level II standard) for inspection.

What We Found

  • Screw hole misalignment — In over 40% of samples, pre-drilled screw holes didn't align with hardware at all. Our inspector spent 15 minutes trying to assemble one sample unit and discovered the door panel simply couldn't be installed.
  • Door panel holes drilled on wrong side — Several cabinet door panels had locker holes drilled on the left side instead of right (or vice versa), making doors impossible to install as designed.
  • Inconsistent hardware quality — Some batches of screws had incomplete threads or shallow Phillips heads that stripped instantly under an electric screwdriver.

Conclusion & Impact

Test results: 0 Critical, 42 Major, 18 Minor defects. The Major defect count far exceeded the AQL acceptance limit (under Level II, 2.5 Major AQL, the acceptance number is 7). The inspection verdict: FAIL.

What would have happened if the client had paid and shipped directly? 2,800 units arriving at a European warehouse — only for the end customer to discover they can't assemble the furniture. Returns, compensation, brand reputation damage. One pre-shipment inspection costing less than 0.3% of the order value saved this client from a potential six-figure Euro loss.

A factory audit in China would likely have uncovered the root cause — this factory's CNC drilling process and jig management had drifted out of tolerance. If identified at the supplier selection stage, the entire batch defect could have been prevented at the source.
