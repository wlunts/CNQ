Chinese New Year 2027 Factory Shutdown Calendar

Real shutdown and restart dates by manufacturing cluster — from CNQ inspectors on the ground in Guangdong, Zhejiang, Fujian and Shandong

2026-09-28  |  Industry Updates  |  By CNQ Inspection Team

Home › Industry Updates › Chinese New Year 2027 Factory Shutdown Calendar

Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday, February 6, 2027. For importers, that single date drives a six-week disruption window: factories stop production well before the official holiday, the world's largest annual human migration empties manufacturing cities, and full capacity does not return until weeks after workers trickle back. This calendar gives you the dates that matter for your production and shipping plan — cluster by cluster, from the inspectors who walk those factory floors every week.

Who this is for: importers, sourcing managers and QA leads with orders that must ship before — or restart after — Chinese New Year 2027. Pair it with our lead time calculator to work out your latest safe order date.

The State Council typically announces the official holiday schedule in November or December. The official holiday, however, is only the middle of the story — factory reality runs longer in both directions. That is exactly what the cluster table in Section 3 captures.

2. Why Chinese New Year Disrupts Supply Chains So Badly

Three forces stack on top of each other:

  1. The migration itself. Hundreds of millions of workers travel home to inland provinces, often 1,000–2,000 km from coastal factory cities. Train and bus capacity is finite, so many workers leave days or weeks before the official holiday — and production lines cannot run without them.
  2. The pre-holiday rush. Every exporter with a China order wants to ship before the holiday, which compresses production schedules, trucking, customs and port capacity into a few overloaded weeks. Overtime and temporary workers keep lines running — and defect rates creep up exactly when you can least afford rework.
  3. The slow, uneven restart. After the holiday, workers return gradually over two to four weeks; some never return at all. New hires need training, first production batches are less stable, and quality issues surface weeks into the "recovery" — often after the first post-holiday shipments have already left.

Our inspectors see the same pattern every year: the highest-risk goods of the calendar are the last containers before CNY and the first containers after. That is not a reason to avoid those windows — it is a reason to inspect them more tightly. A during-production inspection in early January catches rush-order defects while there is still time to rework; a pre-shipment inspection before release protects the last containers out.

5. Quality Risks Around the Holiday — and How to Cover Them

Two windows deserve extra inspection attention every single year:

5.1 The last containers before the holiday

Factories push to ship everything before workers leave. Temporary staff, double shifts and skipped intermediate checks raise defect rates — and rework is impossible once the container is sealed and the line is down for five weeks. Inspect before the container is loaded, not at the port.

5.2 The first batches after the restart

Worker return rates vary by cluster and year; lines run with new hires and re-set machines. The first two to three weeks of post-holiday production are statistically the least stable of the year — yet many importers schedule no inspection at all in that window because "the factory just restarted, we'll give them time." The opposite is the right call: inspect early, while defects are still correctable on the line.

📎 Practical tip: ask your factory for their written shutdown and restart dates in November, then have someone verify in early January that the dates still hold. Factories under-order pressure routinely extend the pre-holiday stop date by a week — quietly. Our factory audit teams collect exactly this kind of ground truth during visits.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

When is Chinese New Year 2027?

Chinese New Year falls on Saturday, February 6, 2027. Factories typically stop production 1–3 weeks before that date and need 2–4 weeks after the holiday to return to full capacity.

How long do Chinese factories close for Chinese New Year?

The official public holiday is expected to run about 8 days around February 6, 2027, but actual factory closures are longer: most production lines stop 1–3 weeks before the holiday, and full staffing and stable output typically take 2–4 weeks after workers return.

When should I place my order to ship before Chinese New Year 2027?

Work backwards from your target ship date. As a rule of thumb, place orders that must ship before CNY by late October to mid-November 2026, and complete pre-shipment inspection no later than mid-January 2027, because trucking, customs and port capacity all tighten sharply in the final two weeks before the holiday.

Why is product quality risk higher before and after Chinese New Year?

Before the holiday, factories rush orders with overtime and temporary workers, which raises defect rates. After the holiday, new or returning workers are still ramping up, so early production runs need tighter inspection. Scheduling during-production and pre-shipment inspections around these windows catches issues before they ship.

Do factories in other Asian countries close for Chinese New Year too?

Yes. Vietnam celebrates Tet on the same lunar calendar, and factories in Vietnam, Cambodia and other parts of Southeast Asia with large Chinese-origin workforces also slow down or close over the same period. Build the holiday into your regional production planning, not just your China planning.
